# An Inertial Proximal Algorithm with Dry Friction: Finite Convergence Results

BRUNO BAJI and ALEXANDRE CABOT

Laboratoire LACO, Université de Limoges, 123 avenue Albert Thomas, 87060 Limoges Cedex,

France. e-mail: baji@tele2.fr, alexandre.cabot@unilim.fr

(Received: 10 May 2004; in final form: 5 August 2005)

Abstract. Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and $A, B: H \Rightarrow H$ two maximal monotone operators. In this paper, we investigate the properties of the following proximal type algorithm:

$$
(x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} + A ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}) + B (x _ {n + 2}) \ni 0, \tag {$\mathcal{A$}}
$$

where $(\lambda_{n})$ is a sequence of positive steps. Algorithm (A) may be viewed as the discretized equation of a nonlinear oscillator subject to friction. We prove that, if $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$ (condition of dry friction), then the sequence $(x_{n})$ generated by (A) is strongly convergent and its limit $x_{\infty}$ satisfies $0 \in A(0) + B(x_{\infty})$. We show that, under a general condition, the limit $x_{\infty}$ is achieved in a finite number of iterations. When this condition is not satisfied, we prove in a rather large setting that the convergence rate is at least geometrical.

Mathematics Subject Classifications (2000): 65K10, 49M25, 70F40.

Key words: proximal point algorithm, maximal monotone operator, dry friction, finite convergence.

# 1. Introduction

In a recent paper, Adly, Attouch and Cabot [1] studied the dynamics of a nonlinear oscillator subject to dry friction via the following differential inclusion:

$$
\ddot {x} (t) + \partial \Phi (\dot {x} (t)) + \nabla f (x (t)) \ni 0, t \geqslant 0, x (t) \in \mathbb {R} ^ {n} \tag {S}
$$

( $\partial$ denotes the subdifferential operator in the sense of convex analysis and $\nabla$ is the classical gradient operator). Equation (S) describes the finite-dimensional motion of a rigid body (of unit mass) sliding on a surface and subject to external forces deriving from the potential f. The term $-\partial\Phi(\dot{x})$ represents the frictional contact of the mass on the surface. When the friction is dry, it is shown in [1] that under general conditions, the equilibrium is reached after a finite time.

For numerical purposes, it is natural to deal with a discretized version of $(S)$. In this paper, we will be especially interested in the following implicit discretization of $(S)$ :

$$
(x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} + \partial \Phi ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}) + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) \ni 0,
$$

where $(\lambda_{n})$ is a sequence of positive steps. The previous algorithm falls into the framework of proximal-like methods. In the whole paper, we extend the study to the general setting of maximal monotone operators in infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces. For that purpose, we consider a Hilbert space H and two maximal monotone operators A, $B: H \Rightarrow H$. We define the sequence $(x_{n})$ by the following recursive algorithm:

$$
(x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} + A ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}) + B (x _ {n + 2}) \ni 0, \tag {$\mathcal{A$}}
$$

When $A(x) = \gamma x$ for some positive $\gamma$ (which corresponds to a linear damping), the algorithm (A) can be rewritten as:

$$
x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} - \alpha_ {n} (x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}) + \beta_ {n} B (x _ {n + 2}) \ni 0, \tag {1}
$$

where the coefficients $\alpha_{n}$ and $\beta_{n}$ are respectively defined by $\alpha_{n}:=1/(1+\gamma\lambda_{n})$ and $\beta_{n}:=\lambda_{n}^{2}/(1+\gamma\lambda_{n})$. When $\alpha_{n}\equiv0$, we recover the standard proximal point algorithm, for which we refer the reader to the abundant literature on this subject [11, 14–16]. On the other hand, when $\alpha_{n}>0$ for some $n\in N$, the extrapolation term $\alpha_{n}(x_{n+1}-x_{n})$ takes into account a kind of inertia associated with the sequence. This situation has been analysed in details in [4] (and also [3] when B is a subdifferential operator), where the algorithm (1) is called the ‘inertial proximal point’ method. The authors prove that, under adequate conditions, the sequence $(x_{n})$ defined by (1) weakly converges to some zero of B. This asymptotic behaviour is strongly related to the fact that $A(0)=\{0\}$.

In this paper, we focus on the situation corresponding to $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$, which can be interpreted as a condition of dry friction. The effect of such an assumption is to force the strong convergence of the sequence $(x_{n})$. The limit point $x_{\infty} := s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_{n}$ satisfies the inclusion $0 \in A(0) + B(x_{\infty})$ : it is an equilibrium point of $(\mathcal{A})$. A central result of the paper consists in deriving a general condition ensuring that the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ is finitely convergent (see Theorem 3.1). The question of finite convergence for proximal methods has been widely studied in the literature (see, for example, [10-13]). It is interesting to note that we need no assumption of linear conditioning as it is often required in this type of results.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we start with a general result of existence and uniqueness of the iterates generated by (A). In Section 3 and the following ones, we assume that $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$ and that the operator $B$ is the subdifferential $\partial f$ of a convex function $f$. We prove the strong convergence of the algorithm (A) toward some equilibrium $x_{\infty} \in H$ satisfying $0 \in A(0) + \partial f(x_{\infty})$.

When moreover the set $-\partial f(x_{\infty})$ is included in the interior of the set $A(0)$, the algorithm (A) is shown to converge in a finite number of iterations (Theorem 3.1). The purpose of the last sections is to analyse the delicate case corresponding to $-\partial f(x_{\infty}) \not\subset \operatorname{int}(A(0))$. We show that the geometry of the set $A(0)$ plays a central role in the asymptotic behaviour of (A). In Section 4 we prove geometrical decay results in the case of a smooth set $A(0)$. Section 5 is concerned with the asymptotic analysis of (A) when the set $A(0)$ is a convex polyhedron.

# 2. Algorithm (A). Preliminary Results

Let us first precise the notations that we use throughout the paper. The Hilbert space $H$ is endowed with the scalar product $\langle ,\rangle$ and the corresponding norm $|\cdot |$. Let $\mathbb{B}$ (resp. $\mathbb{S}$ ) be the closed unit ball of $H$ (resp. the unit sphere) centered at 0. Given a subset $A \subset H$, we denote by $d(., A)$ the distance function to the set $A$ : $d(x, A) = \inf_{y \in A} |x - y|$ for every $x \in H$. Given another subset $B \subset H$, we define the excess $e(A, B)$ of $A$ over $B$ by: $e(A, B) = \sup_{x \in A} d(x, B)$. Throughout the paper, we also use the standard notations of convex analysis. Given a convex function $\Phi: H \to \mathbb{R}$, the set $\partial \Phi(x)$ is the subdifferential set at $x \in H$. The subdifferential of the absolute value function $|\cdot|: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is the set-valued sign function, denoted by $\operatorname{sgn}(x) = 1$ if $x > 0$, $\operatorname{sgn}(x) = -1$ if $x < 0$ and $\operatorname{sgn}(0) = [-1, 1]$. For a convex subset $C \subset H$, we denote by $N_C$ the normal cone operator and by $\sigma_C$ the support function of $C$. The set $\operatorname{conv}(C)$ is the convex hull of $C$. We refer the reader to [17] for the definitions and basic properties relative to these notions.

# 2.1. EXISTENCE AND UNIQUENESS

Consider two maximal monotone operators $A$ : $H \Rightarrow H$ and $B$ : $H \Rightarrow H$. Given initial data $x_0, x_1 \in H$ and a sequence $(\lambda_n)$ of positive steps, let us define the sequence $(x_n)$ by the following algorithm:

$$
\left(x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}\right) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} + A \left(\left(x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}\right) / \lambda_ {n}\right) + B \left(x _ {n + 2}\right) \ni 0. \tag {A}
$$

Algorithm(A) may be viewed as a time discretization of a nonlinear oscillator equation under friction. The linear operator $A(x) = x$ corresponds to a viscous friction whereas the operator A defined by

$$
A (x) = \left\{ \begin{array}{l l} x / | x | & \text {if} x \neq 0, \\ \mathbb {B} & \text {if} x = 0 \end{array} \right.
$$

is associated to the classical Coulomb friction. Notice that the intermediate situation $A(x) = x / |x|^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha \in ]0,1[$ has been studied by Amann and Díaz [5] and Díaz and Liñán [9] in the context of continuous dynamical systems.

We start with a general result of existence and uniqueness of the iterates generated by $(\mathcal{A})$. Let us recall that, for every maximal monotone operator A: $H \Rightarrow H$, the resolvent function $J_{\lambda}^{A} := (I + \lambda A)^{-1}$ is single-valued on H. For a complete account of the theory of maximal monotone operators, we refer the reader to [6, 17].

PROPOSITION 2.1. Assume that $A \colon H \Rightarrow H$ and $B \colon H \Rightarrow H$ are maximal monotone operators satisfying respectively $\operatorname{dom} A = H$ and $\operatorname{dom} B \neq \emptyset$. Given $x_0, x_1 \in H$ and a sequence $(\lambda_n)$ of positive numbers, the sequence $(x_n)$ is uniquely defined by the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ and the initial data $x_0, x_1$.

Proof. Let us define the maximal monotone operator $B_{n}: H \Rightarrow H$ by $B_{n}(x):= B(\lambda_{n}x + x_{n+1})$. The algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ can be rewritten as

$$
\frac {1}{\lambda_ {n}} (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) - \frac {1}{\lambda_ {n}} (x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}) + \lambda_ {n} (A + B _ {n}) ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}) \ni 0,
$$

or equivalently:

$$
(I + \lambda_ {n} (A + B _ {n})) ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}) \ni (x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n}.
$$

Since the sets int (dom A) = H and dom $B_{n}$ have a nonempty intersection, we deduce from [6, Corollary 2.7] that the operator $A + B_{n}$ is maximal monotone. As a consequence, the previous inclusion is equivalent to $(x_{n+2} - x_{n+1})/\lambda_{n} = J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A+B_{n}}((x_{n+1} - x_{n})/\lambda_{n})$, which clearly shows that $x_{n+2}$ is uniquely defined as a function of $x_{n}$ and $x_{n+1}$.

A classical example of monotone operators is given by the subdifferential of convex functions (cf. [6, 17]). Consider a closed convex set C which is bounded, so that the support function $\sigma_{C}$ is closed, convex and satisfies dom $\sigma_{C}=H$. It is immediate to check that the operator $A:=\partial\sigma_{C}$ satisfies the assumptions of Proposition 2.1. This remark gives rise to the following examples, obtained by particularizing the set C.

EXAMPLE 2.1. Take $H = \mathbb{R}^n$ and let $L \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be a symmetric positive definite matrix. Define the set $C := \mathbb{B}_L = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \langle Lx, x \rangle \leqslant 1\}$ and the associated norm $|\cdot|_L$ given by $|x|_L = \sqrt{\langle Lx, x \rangle}$. It is easy to check that, for every $x \in H$, $\sigma_{\mathbb{B}_L}(x) = \sqrt{\langle L^{-1}x, x \rangle} = |x|_{L^{-1}}$. An immediate computation shows that $\sigma_{\mathbb{B}_L}$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$ and that:

$$
\forall x \in \mathbb {R} ^ {n} \backslash \{0 \}, \quad \nabla \sigma_ {\mathbb {B} _ {L}} (x) = \frac {L ^ {- 1} x}{\sqrt {\langle L ^ {- 1} x , x \rangle}}.
$$

The subdifferential set of $\sigma_{B_{L}}$, at 0 equals $\partial\sigma_{\mathbb{B}_{L}}(0)=\mathbb{B}_{L}$.

EXAMPLE 2.2. Take $H = \mathbb{R}^n$ and $C := \mathbb{B}_p = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|^p \leqslant 1\}$ for some $p \in ]1, \infty[$. Let $q$ be the conjugate exponent of $p$ defined by $1/p + 1/q = 1$.

It is well-known that for all $x \in H$, $\sigma_C(x) = |x|_q = \left(\sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|^q\right)^{1/q}$. An easy computation shows that $\partial |\cdot|_q$ is given by

$$
\partial | \cdot | _ {q} (x) &= \left\{ \begin{aligned} \frac {1}{| x | _ {q} ^ {q / p}} \left(| x _ {1} | ^ {q - 1} \mathrm{sgn} \left(x _ {1}\right), \dots , | x _ {n} | ^ {q - 1} \mathrm{sgn} \left(x _ {n}\right)\right) \text {if} x \neq 0, \\ \mathbb {B} _ {p} \text {if} x &= 0. \end{aligned} \right.
$$

EXAMPLE 2.3. Take $H = \mathbb{R}^n$ and $C := \mathbb{B}_{\infty} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \max_{i=1}^n |x_i| \leqslant 1\}$. We classically have for all $x \in H$, $\sigma_C(x) = |x|_1 = \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|$ and it is then immediate that $\partial|\cdot|_1(x) = (\text{sgn}(x_1), \ldots, \text{sgn}(x_n))$. In particular, we have $\partial|\cdot|_1(0) = \mathbb{B}_{\infty}$.

EXAMPLE 2.4. Take $H = \mathbb{R}^n$ and $C := \mathbb{B}_1 = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i| \leqslant 1\}$. It is classical that for all $x \in H$, $\sigma_C(x) = |x|_\infty = \max_{i=1}^n |x_i|$ and since $|\cdot|_\infty$ is the supremum of a finite collection of convex functions, the usual rule of sub-differentiation yields:

$$
\partial | \cdot | _ {\infty} (x) = \left\{ \begin{array}{c c} \operatorname{conv} \left\{\operatorname{sgn} \left(x _ {i}\right) e _ {i}, i \in I _ {x} \right\} & \text {if} x \neq 0, \\ \mathbb {B} _ {1} & \text {if} x = 0, \end{array} \right.
$$

where $(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})$ is the canonical basis of $R^{n}$ and $I_{x}=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},|x_{i}|=|x|_{\infty}\}$ is the set of active indices.

The previous examples will serve us as a guideline throughout the paper.

# 2.2. ENERGY DECAY

Let us now assume that the operator B is the subdifferential of some convex function $f: H \to R \cup \{+\infty\}$. In this case, the algorithm (A) may appear as an implicit discretization of a nonlinear oscillator subject to forces deriving from the potential f. Due to this mechanical interpretation, let us define the energy-like sequence $(E_{n})$ by

$$
E _ {n} = \frac {1}{2 \lambda_ {n} ^ {2}} | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | ^ {2} + f (x _ {n + 1}). \tag {2}
$$

It is easy to check that the sequence $(E_{n})$ is nonincreasing. More precisely, we will prove next that its decay rate is given by the function $\Phi_{A}$ defined as follows:

$$
\Phi_ {A} (x) := \inf _ {y \in A (x)} \langle y, x \rangle . \tag {3}
$$

The next lemma summarizes the main properties of $\Phi_{A}$.

LEMMA 2.1. Let $A: H \Rightarrow H$ be a monotone operator such that $\operatorname{dom} A = H$ and $0 \in A(0)$. The function $\Phi_A$ defined by (3) satisfies:

(i) $\forall x\in H,\Phi_A(x)\geqslant \sigma_{A(0)}(x)\geqslant 0.$  
(ii) $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0)) \Rightarrow \exists \alpha > 0, \quad \forall x \in H, \Phi_A(x) \geqslant \alpha |x|$ .

# Proof.

(i) For any $x \in H$, let $y \in A(x)$ and $z \in A(0)$. The monotonicity of $A$ yields $\langle y - z, x - 0 \rangle \geqslant 0$, and hence $\langle y, x \rangle \geqslant \langle z, x \rangle$. Since this is true for every $y \in A(x)$ and $z \in A(0)$, we infer that

$$
\Phi_ {A} (x) := \inf _ {y \in A (x)} \langle y, x \rangle \geqslant \sup _ {z \in A (0)} \langle z, x \rangle = \sigma_ {A (0)} (x).
$$

On the other hand, since $0 \in A(0)$, it is clear that $\sigma_{A(0)}(x) \geqslant 0$.

(ii) According to a classical result, the assertion $0 \in \operatorname{int}(C)$ (where $C \subset H$ ) is equivalent to the existence of $\alpha > 0$ such that $\sigma_C \geqslant \alpha |\cdot|$. The conclusion is then an immediate consequence of (i).

Remark 2.1. In the particular case where $A := \partial g$, conclusion (i) of Lemma 2.1 can be slightly improved. Indeed, the subdifferential inequality yields:

$$
\forall x \in H, \quad \Phi_ {\partial g} (x) \geqslant g (x) - g (0) \geqslant \sigma_ {\partial g (0)} (x). \tag {4}
$$

The following proposition shows that the sequence $(E_{n})$ is non-increasing and expresses its decay rate as a function of $\Phi_{A}$.

PROPOSITION 2.2. Let $A \colon H \Rightarrow H$ be a maximal monotone operator such that $\operatorname{dom} A = H$ and $0 \in A(0)$. The operator $B$ is supposed to be equal to $\partial f$ for a closed convex function $f \colon H \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ satisfying $\operatorname{dom} \partial f \neq \emptyset$ and $\inf f > -\infty$. The positive sequence $(\lambda_n)$ is assumed to be nondecreasing. Then, the sequence $(x_n)$ defined by algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ satisfies:

(i) $E_{n + 1} - E_n \leqslant -\lambda_n \Phi_A((x_{n + 2} - x_{n + 1}) / \lambda_n)$, where the sequence $(E_n)$ and the function $\Phi_A$ are defined by (2) and (3), respectively.

(ii) $\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda_n\Phi_A((x_{n+2}-x_{n+1})/\lambda_n) < +\infty.$

Proof. (i) Let $v_{n+2} \in \partial f(x_{n+2})$ satisfy

$$
(x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} + v _ {n + 2} \in - A ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}). \tag {5}
$$

Since $\lambda_{n}\leqslant \lambda_{n + 1}$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned} E _ {n + 1} - E _ {n} &\leqslant \frac {1}{2 \lambda_ {n} ^ {2}} \left(\left| x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right| ^ {2} - \left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \right| ^ {2}\right) + f (x _ {n + 2}) - f (x _ {n + 1}) \\ &\leqslant \frac {1}{\lambda_ {n} ^ {2}} \left\langle x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}, x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right\rangle + f (x _ {n + 2}) - f (x _ {n + 1}). \\ \end{aligned}
$$

The convexity of $f$ gives $f(x_{n + 2}) - f(x_{n + 1}) \leqslant \langle v_{n + 2}, x_{n + 2} - x_{n + 1} \rangle$, so that the previous inequality yields

$$
E _ {n + 1} - E _ {n} \leqslant \big \langle (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} + v _ {n + 2}, x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \big \rangle .
$$

In view of inclusion (5) and the definition of $\Phi_A$, we immediately deduce

$$
E _ {n + 1} - E _ {n} \leqslant - \lambda_ {n} \Phi_ {A} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}). \tag {6}
$$

(ii) Let us add inequalities (6) for $n = 0, 1, \ldots, N$

$$
\sum_ {n = 0} ^ {N} \lambda_ {n} \Phi_ {A} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}) \leqslant E _ {0} - E _ {N + 1} \leqslant E _ {0} - \inf f.
$$

Taking the limit when $N \rightarrow +\infty$ gives the expected conclusion.

Let us now apply the result of Proposition 2.2 to the case $A := \partial|\cdot|^{\alpha}$, for some $\alpha \geqslant 1$. In the sequel, $l^{\alpha}$ denotes the set of nonnegative sequences $(a_{n})$ satisfying $\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} a_{n}^{\alpha} < +\infty$.

COROLLARY 2.1. Let $f: H \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ be a closed convex function satisfying $\operatorname{dom} \partial f \neq 0$ and $\inf f > -\infty$. The operators $A$ and $B$ are supposed to be respectively defined by $A := \partial |\cdot|^{\alpha} (\alpha \geqslant 1)$ and $B := \partial f$. Consider a non decreasing sequence $(\lambda_n)$ of positive steps. Then, the sequence $(x_n)$ defined by $(\mathcal{A})$ satisfies $|(x_{n+2} - x_{n+1}) / \lambda_n| \in l^\alpha$.

Proof. From Proposition 2.2(ii) and expression (4), we obtain

$$
\sum_ {n = 0} ^ {+ \infty} \lambda_ {n} | (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n} | ^ {\alpha} <   + \infty .
$$

The conclusion follows from the fact that $\lambda_{n} \geqslant \lambda_{0} > 0$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

The case $\alpha = 1$ is specially interesting in the sense that $(|x_{n + 1} - x_n|) \in l^1$ implies the strong convergence of the sequence $(x_n)$. This remark is exploited in the next section, giving rise to a capital result of the paper.

# 3. Dry Friction Condition: $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$

In what follows, we will suppose that $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$. From the mechanical point of view, the assumption $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$ can be interpreted as a condition of dry friction. Examples of nonlinear oscillators subject to such a friction are given in [2, 7]. The fundamental aspect of the condition $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$ is the finite convergence of the associate algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$. According to the classical terminology, a sequence is said to be finitely convergent if it converges in a finite number of iterations. The question of finite convergence for proximal-like algorithms has given rise to an abundant literature (see for example [10-13]). Let us recall that the function $f: H \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ is linearly conditioned if $\arg\min f \neq \emptyset$ and there exists $\gamma > 0$ such that

$$
\forall x \in H, \quad f (x) \geqslant \min f + \gamma d (x, \operatorname{argmin} f)
$$

It is well known that the standard proximal method is finitely convergent for linearly conditioned convex functions. Our result does not require this kind of assumption. Here, the finite convergence of $(\mathcal{A})$ toward $x_{\infty}$, is obtained under a mild condition on $x_{\infty}$. This result is strongly related to the fact that oscillators subject to dry friction are stabilized in a finite time. We refer the reader to [1] where a continuous version of $(\mathcal{A})$ is studied in the particular case $A = \partial\Phi$ and $B = \nabla f$ (with $\Phi, f: H \to R$ respectively convex and differentiable).

In the following statement, we denote by $\mathcal{D}$ the set $\{n\in\mathbb{N},x_{n+1}=x_n\}$, where $(x_{n})$ is the sequence defined by algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$.

THEOREM 3.1. Let $A: H \Rightarrow H$ be a maximal monotone operator such that $\text{dom } A = H$ and $0 \in \text{int} (A(0))$. The operator $B$ is supposed to be equal to $\partial f$ for a closed convex function $f: H \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ satisfying $\text{dom } \partial f \neq \emptyset$ and $\inf f > -\infty$. The positive sequence $(\lambda_n)$ is assumed to be nondecreasing. Then, the sequence $(x_n)$ defined by algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ satisfies:

(i) $(|x_{n+1} - x_n|) \in l^1$ and therefore $x_\infty := s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_n$ exists.  
(ii) The vector $x_{\infty}$ is such that $0 \in A(0) + \partial f(x_{\infty})$.  
(iii) If $H$ is finite-dimensional, then either the sequence $(x_{n})$ is finitely convergent or the limit points of $\left(\frac{x_{n+1} - x_n}{|x_{n+1} - x_n|}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{D}}$ are contained in

$$
N _ {A (0)} (- \partial f (x _ {\infty})) := \bigcup_ {\xi \in - \partial f (x _ {\infty})} N _ {A (0)} (\xi).
$$

If moreover $-\partial f(x_{\infty}) \subset \operatorname{int}(A(0))$, the sequence $(x_n)$ is finitely convergent.

Proof. (i) From Lemma 2.1 (ii), the assumption $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$ implies that $\Phi_A \geqslant \alpha|\cdot|$. for some $\alpha > 0$. Assertion (i) is then an immediate consequence of Proposition 2.2.

(ii) Let $v_{n+2} \in \partial f(x_{n+2})$ satisfy:

$$
\left. - (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} - v _ {n + 2} \in A ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}). \right. \tag {7}
$$

Since $s - \lim_{n\to +\infty}x_n = x_\infty$ and since $\lambda_{n}\geqslant \underline{\lambda} >0$ for every $n\in \mathbb{N}$, we have

$$
s - \lim _ {n \to + \infty} (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} = 0. \tag {8}
$$

On the other hand, since $\operatorname{dom} A = H$, the operator $A$ is locally bounded on $H$, which clearly implies that the sequence $(v_n)$ is bounded in $H$. Therefore, there exist $\overline{v} \in H$ and a subsequence of $(v_n)$, still denoted by $(v_n)$ such that $w - \lim_{n \to +\infty} v_n = \overline{v}$. Since the graph of $\partial f$ is closed for the product topology $s - H \times w - H$, we have $\overline{v} \in \partial f(x_\infty)$. In view of (7) and (8), the same graph closedness property applied to $A$ shows that $-\overline{v} \in A(0)$. We then conclude that $0 \in A(0) + \partial f(x_\infty)$.

(iii) Now assume that the space $H$ is finite-dimensional and that the sequence $(x_{n})$ is not finitely convergent. Let $\overline{\omega}$ denote the (nonempty) set of limit points of $\left(\frac{x_{n+1}-x_n}{|x_{n+1}-x_n|}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{D}}$ and let $d\in\overline{\omega}$. There exists a subsequence of $(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{D}}$, still denoted by $(x_{n})$ such that $\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{x_{n+1}-x_n}{|x_{n+1}-x_n|}=d$. Let $v_{n+2}\in\partial f(x_{n+2})$ satisfy the same relation as above. The same arguments as in (ii) show that there exist $\overline{v}\in H$ and a subsequence of $(v_{n})$, still denoted by $(v_{n})$ such that $\lim_{n\to+\infty}v_{n}=\overline{v}\in\partial f(x_{\infty})$. Let $\xi$ be an element of $A(0)$ ; from the monotonicity of $A$, we have

$$
\left\langle - (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) \Big / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} - v _ {n + 2} - \xi , \frac {x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}}{\left| x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right|} \right\rangle \geqslant 0.
$$

By taking the limit in the previous inequality when $n \to +\infty$, we obtain $\langle -\overline{v} - \xi, d \rangle \geqslant 0$, which expresses that $d \in N_{A(0)}(-\overline{v}) \subset N_{A(0)}(-\partial f(x_{\infty}))$. Let us now assume that $-\partial f(x_{\infty}) \subset \operatorname{int}(A(0))$ and let us prove that the sequence $(x_n)$ is finitely convergent. We argue by contradiction and we still denote by $\overline{\omega}$ the set of limit points of $\left(\frac{x_{n+1} - x_n}{|x_{n+1} - x_n|}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \mathcal{D}}$. In view of $-\partial f(x_{\infty}) \subset \operatorname{int}(A(0))$, we have $\overline{\omega} \subset N_{A(0)}(-\partial f(x_{\infty})) = \{0\}$. Hence 0 is the unique limit point of the bounded sequence $\left(\frac{x_{n+1} - x_n}{|x_{n+1} - x_n|}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \mathcal{D}}$ and we conclude that $\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{x_{n+1} - x_n}{|x_{n+1} - x_n|} = 0$. This clearly contradicts the fact that $\frac{x_{n+1} - x_n}{|x_{n+1} - x_n|} \in \mathbb{S}$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Let us illustrate Theorem 3.1 by a few one-dimensional examples.

EXAMPLE 3.1. Take $H = \mathbb{R}$ and define the mappings $A$ and $f$ respectively by $A(x) = a \operatorname{sgn}(x)$ and $f(x) = bx^2 / 2$ where $a$ and $b$ are positive coefficients. The limit $x_{\infty}$ must fulfil the inclusion $0 \in a[-1, 1] + b x_{\infty}$, or equivalently $x_{\infty} \in [-a / b, a / b]$. If, moreover, $x_{\infty} \in ] -a / b, a / b[$, Theorem 3.1 (iii) asserts that the sequence $(x_n)$ finitely converges to $x_{\infty}$.

EXAMPLE 3.2. Take $H = \mathbb{R}$ and define the mappings $A$ and $f$ respectively by $A(x) = a \operatorname{sgn}(x)$ and $f(x) = b |x|$ where $a$ and $b$ are positive coefficients. The condition $0 \in A(0) + \partial f(x_{\infty})$ can be rewritten as: $0 \in a[-1, 1] + b \operatorname{sgn}(x_{\infty})$. If $b > a$, the unique solution of the previous inclusion is $x_{\infty} = 0$ so that the sequence $(x_n)$ converges toward 0. Now assume that $b \leqslant a$. It is then elementary to check that every $x_{\infty} \in \mathbb{R}$ is solution of $0 \in A(0) + \partial f(x_{\infty})$ : the sequence $(x_n)$ may converge toward any element of $\mathbb{R}$. If, moreover, $b < a$, the convergence is finite.

Let us give a counterexample to finite convergence when $-\partial f(x_{\infty}) \not\subset \text{int}(A(0))$.

EXAMPLE 3.3. Take $H = \mathbb{R}$ and define the operator $A$ (resp. the function $f$ ) by $A(x) = \operatorname{sgn}(x) + 2x$ (resp. $f(x) = x^2 / 2$ ). If the sequence $(\lambda_n)$ is constant and equal to 1, the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ reduces to

$$
x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n} + \operatorname{sgn} \left(x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}\right) + 2 (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) + x _ {n + 2} \ni 0,
$$

or, equivalently,

$$
4 x _ {n + 2} - 4 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n} \in - \mathrm{sgn} (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}).
$$

Take as initial data $x_0 = x_1 = 2$. We let the reader check that the expression of $x_n$ is given by $x_n = 1 + (1 + n)/2^n$, so that $(x_n)$ converges toward $x_\infty := 1$. There is no finite convergence but simply geometrical convergence, because of $-1 = -f'(1) \notin \operatorname{int}(A(0)) = ] - 1, 1[$.

In the next sections, we will focus on the particular case where the function $f$ is smooth. In this situation, Theorem 3.1 shows that finite convergence holds as soon as $-\nabla f(x_{\infty}) \notin \mathrm{bd}(A(0))$. Since the boundary of the convex set $A(0)$ has an empty interior, it is reasonable to think that the circumstances leading to $-\nabla f(x_{\infty}) \in \mathrm{bd}(A(0))$ are 'exceptional'. The purpose of the next two sections is to precise the asymptotic properties of $(\mathcal{A})$ in this case, which will be referred to as the 'boundary case'. The corresponding study for the continuous dynamical system $(S)$ has been performed by the second author in the recent paper [8].

# 4. Analysis of the Boundary Case (I): A(0) Smooth

Throughout this section, we assume that $H$ is finite-dimensional. Let us recall that a convex set $C \subset H$ is said to be smooth at a point $\overline{x} \in C$ if $N_C(\overline{x})$ is reduced to a half-line. The set $C$ is said to be smooth if it is smooth at every point of its boundary.

# 4.1. ANALYSIS OF THE MODE OF CONVERGENCE

The next result shows that if the set $A(0)$ is smooth then, either the sequence $(x_{n})$ generated by $(\mathcal{A})$ is finitely convergent or the direction of the vector differences $x_{n+1} - x_n$, tends to be normal to the boundary of $A(0)$ when $n \to +\infty$.

PROPOSITION 4.1. Let $H$ be a finite dimensional space and let $A: H \Rightarrow H$ be a maximal monotone operator such that $\operatorname{dom} A = H$ and $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$. The operator $B$ is supposed to be equal to $\nabla f$ for a convex function $f: H \to \mathbb{R}$ of class $\mathcal{C}^1$ satisfying $\inf f > -\infty$. The positive sequence $(\lambda_n)$ is assumed to be non decreasing. Let $(x_n)$ be the sequence generated by the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ and let us set $x_\infty = \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_n$. Assume that the set $A(0)$ is smooth at the point $-\nabla f(x_\infty)$ and consider the unique vector $u \in H$ defined by $|u| = 1$ and $N_{A(0)}(-\nabla f(x_\infty)) = \mathbb{R}_+ u$. Then, either the sequence $(x_n)$ is finitely convergent or we have

$$
\lim _ {n \rightarrow + \infty , n \in \mathbb {N} \backslash \mathcal {D}} \frac {x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}}{\left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \right|} = u, \tag {9}
$$

$$
x _ {n} = x _ {\infty} - \left(\sum_ {k = n} ^ {+ \infty} \left| x _ {k + 1} - x _ {k} \right|\right) u + \mathrm{o} \left(\sum_ {k = n} ^ {+ \infty} \left| x _ {k + 1} - x _ {k} \right|\right). \tag {10}
$$

As a consequence, we have the equivalence $|x_{n} - x_{\infty}| \sim \sum_{k=n}^{+\infty} |x_{k+1} - x_{k}|$ when $n \to +\infty$ and we deduce that, either $x_{n} = x_{\infty}$ for $n$ large enough or $|x_{n} - x_{\infty}| > 0$ for $n$ large enough.

Proof. Let $\overline{\omega}$ denote the (nonempty) set of limit points of $\left(\frac{x_{n+1}-x_n}{|x_{n+1}-x_n|}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{D}}$.

From Theorem 3.1 (iii), we have $\overline{\omega} \subset N_{A(0)}(-\nabla f(x_{\infty})) = \mathbb{R}_{+}u$. On the other hand, we have $\frac{x_{n+1}-x_n}{|x_{n+1}-x_n|} \in \mathbb{S}$, and therefore $\overline{\omega} \subset \mathbb{R}_+u \cap \mathbb{S} = \{u\}$. Hence, $u$ is the unique limit point of the bounded sequence $\left(\frac{x_{n+1}-x_n}{|x_{n+1}-x_n|}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \mathcal{D}}$ and we conclude that $\lim \left(\frac{x_{n+1}-x_n}{|x_{n+1}-x_n|}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \mathcal{D}} = u$ or, equivalently,

$$
x _ {n + 1} - X _ {n} = | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | u + \mathrm{o} (| \mathrm{x} _ {\mathrm{n+1}} - \mathrm{x} _ {\mathrm{n}})
$$

when $n \to +\infty$. Summing the last equality from $n = N$ to $+\infty$ and taking into account the fact that $(|x_{n+1} - x_n|)_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \in l^1$, we obtain (10). The other assertions are immediate consequences of Equation (10).

# 4.1. ESTIMATION OF THE CONVERGENCE RATE

Let us recall that a function $f \colon H \to \mathbb{R}$ is said to be of class $\mathcal{C}^{1,1}$ if it is of class $\mathcal{C}^1$ and if its gradient $\nabla f$ is locally Lipschitz on $H$. On the other hand, a $\mathcal{C}^1$ function $f \colon H \to \mathbb{R}$ is said to be strongly convex if there exists $M > 0$ such that

$$
\forall x, y \in H, \langle \nabla f (x) - \nabla f (y), x - y \rangle \geqslant M | x - y | ^ {2}. \tag {11}
$$

Let us also recall that the excess of the set $C$ over the set $D$ is measured by the quantity $e(C, D) := \sup_{x \in C} d(x, D)$. In the next theorem, the operator $A$ is assumed to verify: there exist $\eta > 0$ and $\alpha \geqslant 0$ such that

$$
| x | \leqslant \eta \Rightarrow e (A (x), A (0)) \leqslant \alpha | x |. \tag {12}
$$

Roughly speaking, this condition means that the growth of the set $A(x)$ with respect to the set $A(0)$ is at most linear in the neighbourhood of 0. The next theorem shows that under a smoothness assumption on the set $A(0)$, either the sequence $(x_{n})$ generated by $(\mathcal{A})$ finitely converges or the convergence rate is geometrical. This result is the discrete version of [8, Theorem 3.2] where the trajectory is proved to converge exponentially toward its limit.

THEOREM 4.1. Let $H$ be a finite dimensional space and let $A: H \Rightarrow H$ be a maximal monotone operator such that $\operatorname{dom} A = H$ and $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$. Assume that there exist $\eta > 0$ and $\alpha \geqslant 0$ such that the linear growth condition (12) holds. Suppose that the function $f: H \to \mathbb{R}$ is of class $\mathcal{C}^{1,1}$, bounded from below and satisfies the strong convexity assumption (11); the operator $B$ is defined by $B := \nabla f$. The sequence $(\lambda_n)$ is assumed to be constant, equal to $\lambda > 0$. Let $(x_n)$ be the

sequence generated by the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ and let us set $x_{\infty} = \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_n$. If the set $A(0)$ is smooth at the point $-\nabla f(x_{\infty})$, then one of the following cases holds:

(i) The sequence $(x_{n})$ is finitely convergent.  
(ii) There exist $n_0 \in \mathbb{N}, C_1, C_2 > 0$ and $q_1, q_2 \in ]0, 1[$ such that for every $n \geqslant n_0$,

$$
| x _ {n} - x _ {\infty} | \geqslant C _ {1} q _ {1} ^ {n} \quad a n d \quad \sum_ {k = n} ^ {+ \infty} | x _ {k} - x _ {\infty} | \leqslant C _ {2} q _ {2} ^ {n}.
$$

Any $q_1 \in ]0, 1[$ (resp. $q_2 \in ]0, 1[$ ) such that $q_1 < 1 / (\alpha \lambda + 1)$ (resp. $q_2 > \alpha / (\alpha + M\lambda)$ ) satisfies the previous estimate. If moreover $\alpha < \sqrt{M}$ then the convergence is finite.

Proof. Let us assume that case (i) does not hold, i.e., the sequence $(x_{n})$ does not finitely converge. Let us define $\xi_{n}$ as the unique element of $A(0)$ such that

$$
\begin{aligned} d \bigl (- (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} - \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}), A (0) \bigr) \\ &= \left| \xi_ {n} + (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) \right|. \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Since the set $A(0)$ is smooth at the point $-\nabla f(x_{\infty})$, there exists $u \in H$ such that $|u| = 1$ and $N_{A(0)}(-\nabla f(x_{\infty})) = \mathbb{R}_{+}u$. We then have

$$
\begin{aligned} \left\langle (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2}, u \right\rangle \\ &= \left\langle \xi_ {n} + (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}), u \right\rangle + \\ + \langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), - u \rangle - \langle \xi_ {n} + \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), u \rangle . \tag {13} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Let us now minorize each term of the right member. From the definitions of the term $\xi_{n}$ and the sequence $(x_{n})$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned} \left| \xi_ {n} + (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) \right| \\ &\leqslant e (A ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda), A (0)). \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Since $\lim_{n\to +\infty}x_{n + 2} - x_{n + 1} = 0$, we deduce from the assumption (12) that, for $n$ large enough

$$
\left| \xi_ {n} + (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) \right| \leqslant \alpha / \lambda | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} |. \tag {14}
$$

Since $u \in N_{A(0)}(-\nabla f(x_{\infty}))$ and $\xi_n \in A(0)$, we infer

$$
\langle \xi_ {n} + \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), u \rangle \leqslant 0. \tag {15}
$$

From Proposition 4.1, we have $x_{n+2} - x_{\infty} = -|x_{n+2} - x_{\infty}| u + o(|x_{n+2} - x_{\infty}|)$ when $n \to +\infty$ and $|x_{n+2} - x_{\infty}| > 0$ for $n$ large enough. We deduce that

$$
\langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), - u \rangle = \bigg \langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), \frac {x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty}}{| x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} |} + \mathrm{o} (1) \bigg \rangle .
$$

Hence, in view of the strong convexity assumption (11) and the local Lipschitz continuity of $\nabla f$, we infer that

$$
\langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), - u \rangle \geqslant M | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} | + o (| x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} |).
$$

Let us set $R_{n}=\sum_{k=n+1}^{+\infty}|x_{k+1}-x_{k}|$ ; from Proposition 4.1, we have the equivalence $|x_{n+1}-x_{\infty}|\sim R_{n}$. As a consequence, the previous inequality can be rewritten as

$$
\langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), - u \rangle \geqslant M R _ {n + 1} + \mathrm{o} (R _ {n + 1}). \tag {16}
$$

In view of equality (13), we deduce from inequalities (14), (15) and (16) that

$$
\begin{aligned} \left\langle (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2}, u \right\rangle \tag {17} \\ &\geqslant - \alpha / \lambda | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} | + M R _ {n + 1} + \mathrm{o} (R _ {n + 1}). \\ \end{aligned}
$$

It ensues that $(R_{n})\in l^{1}$ so that we can define $S_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{+\infty}R_{k+1}$. Now, let us sum inequalities (17) from k=n to $+\infty$ to obtain

$$
- \big \langle (x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2}, u \big \rangle \geqslant - \alpha R _ {n} / \lambda + M S _ {n} + \mathrm{o} (S _ {n}).
$$

Since $x_{n+1} - x_n = |x_{n+1} - x_n|$ $u + o(|x_{n+1} - x_n|)$, we infer:

$$
| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | / \lambda^ {2} + \mathrm{o} (| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} |) + M S _ {n} + \mathrm{o} (S _ {n}) \leqslant \alpha R _ {n} / \lambda .
$$

As a consequence, for every $\varepsilon \in ]0$, 1[ and for $n$ large enough, we have:

$$
(1 - \varepsilon) | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | / \lambda^ {2} \leqslant \alpha R _ {n} / \lambda \quad \text { and } \quad (1 - \varepsilon) M S _ {n} \leqslant \alpha R _ {n} / \lambda .
$$

By noting that $R_{n-1} - R_n = |x_{n+1} - x_n|$, the first inequality yields for $n$ large enough:

$$
R _ {n} \geqslant \left(\frac {1 - \varepsilon}{\alpha \lambda + 1 - \varepsilon}\right) R _ {n - 1}.
$$

Therefore, there exists $n_1 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for every $n \geqslant n_1, R_n \geqslant \left( \frac{1 - \varepsilon}{\alpha\lambda + 1 - \varepsilon} \right)^{n - n_1} R_{n_1}$. Hence, by using the equivalence $R_n \sim |x_{n+1} - x_\infty|$, we infer that there exists $C_1 > 0$ such that for $n$ large enough:

$$
\left| x _ {n} - x _ {\infty} \right| \geqslant C _ {1} \left(\frac {1 - \varepsilon}{\alpha \lambda + 1 - \varepsilon}\right) ^ {n} = C _ {1} q _ {1} ^ {n}. \tag {18}
$$

In the same way, we have, $S_{n-1} - S_n = R_n$, and the second inequality gives

$$
S _ {n} \leqslant \left(\frac {\alpha}{\lambda (1 - \varepsilon) M + \alpha}\right) S _ {n - 1}.
$$

As a consequence, there exists $n_2 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for every $n \geqslant n_2, S_n \leqslant \left( \frac{\alpha}{\lambda(1 - \varepsilon)M + \alpha} \right)^{n - n_2} S_{n_2}$. We can conclude that there exists $C_2 > 0$ such that for $n$ large enough:

$$
\sum_ {k = n} ^ {+ \infty} | x _ {k} - x _ {\infty} | \leqslant C _ {2} \left(\frac {\alpha}{\lambda (1 - \varepsilon) M + \alpha}\right) ^ {n} = C _ {2} q _ {2} ^ {n}. \tag {19}
$$

Let us now assume that case (ii) holds. If $\alpha = 0$, inequality (19) shows that $x_{n} = x_{\infty}$, for $n$ large enough, which contradicts the infinite convergence assumption. We thus infer that $\alpha > 0$. By adding inequalities (18) from $n$ to $+\infty$, we obtain: $\sum_{k=n}^{+\infty}|x_k - x_\infty| \geqslant C_1 / (1 - q_1)q_1^n$. By comparing with inequality (19), we infer that $q_1 \leqslant q_2$ or equivalently $\alpha^2 \geqslant (1 - \varepsilon)^2 M$. This inequality being true for every $\varepsilon \in ]0,1[$, we conclude that $\alpha^2 \geqslant M$ i.e., $\alpha \geqslant \sqrt{M}$.

In the next corollary, we particularize Theorem 4.1 to the case where $A := \partial \sigma_{C} + A_{0}$, $A_{0}$ being a single-valued operator such that $A_{0}(0) = 0$.

COROLLARY 4.1. Let $H$ be a finite dimensional space and let $C$ be a closed convex subset of $H$ which is bounded and such that $0 \in \operatorname{int}(C)$. Consider a single-valued maximal monotone operator $A_0 \colon H \to H$ and assume that there exist $\eta > 0$ and $\alpha \geqslant 0$ such that

$$
| x | \leqslant \eta \Rightarrow | A _ {0} (x) | \leqslant \alpha | x |. \tag {20}
$$

Define the maximal monotone operator $A$ : $H \Rightarrow H$ by $A := \partial \sigma_{C} + A_{0}$. The operator $B$ and the sequence $(\lambda_{n})$ satisfy the same assumptions as in Theorem 4.1. Let $(x_{n})$ be the sequence generated by the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ and let us set $x_{\infty} = \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_{n}$. If the set $C$ is smooth at the point $-\nabla f(x_{\infty})$, then the sequence $(x_{n})$ satisfies the same conclusions as in Theorem 4.1.

Proof. It is immediate to check that $\partial\sigma_{C}(0)=C$ and $\partial\sigma_{C}(x)\subset C$ for every $x\in H$. From the assumption (20), we deduce that $A_{0}(0)=0$ and, hence, $A(0)=C$. Let us verify that condition (12) of Theorem 4.1 holds. Indeed, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned} e (A (x), A (0)) &= e (\partial \sigma_ {C} (x) + A _ {0} (x), C) \\ &\leqslant e (C + A _ {0} (x), C) \\ &= \sup _ {\xi \in C} d (\xi + A _ {0} (x), C) \\ &\leqslant \sup _ {\xi \in C} | \xi + A _ {0} (x) - \xi | = | A _ {0} (x) |. \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, in view of assumption (20), we conclude that: $|x| \leqslant \eta \Rightarrow e(A(x), A(0)) \leqslant \alpha|x|$. It suffices then to apply Theorem 4.1. ☐

If the set $C$ is smooth at every point of its boundary, the conclusions of the previous corollary automatically hold without condition on $x_{\infty}$. This is for

example the case when $C = \mathbb{B}_L$ (see Example 2.1) or $C = \mathbb{B}_p$ for some $p \in ]1, +\infty[$ (see Example 2.2).

# 5. Analysis of the Boundary Case (II): $A(0)$ Convex Polyhedron

In this section, H is a general Hilbert space. We now focus our attention on the case where the set $A(0)$ is a convex polyhedron. The key property that will be exploited next is the following one: let $C \subset H$ be a convex polyhedron and $\bar{x} \in C$. Then, there exists a neighbourhood V of $\bar{x}$ such that

$$
\forall x \in V, \quad N _ {C} (x) \subset N _ {C} (\overline {{x}}). \tag {21}
$$

This property will play a crucial role in the analysis of the asymptotic behaviour of $(\mathcal{A})$.

# 5.1. ANALYSIS OF THE MODE OF CONVERGENCE

Denoting by $(x_{n})$ the sequence generated by $(\mathcal{A})$, the next result shows that the vector differences $x_{n+1} - x_{n}$ are normal to the boundary of $A(0)$ for n large enough. The meaning of such a result is clear with the interpretation of $(x_{n+1} - x_{n})/\lambda_{n}$ as a discrete velocity. We need the following technical assumption

$$
\lim _ {x \to 0} e \big (A (x), \partial \sigma_ {A (0)} (x) \big) = 0. \tag {22}
$$

It is immediate to check that this assumption holds when $A := \partial\sigma_{C} + A_{0}$, for some convex set $C \subset H$ and some single-valued operator $A_{0}: H \to H$ such that $\lim_{x \to 0} A_{0}(x) = 0$.

PROPOSITION 5.1. Let $A: H \Rightarrow H$ be a maximal monotone operator such that $\operatorname{dom} A = H$ and $0 \in \operatorname{int}(A(0))$. Assume that the set $A(0)$ is a convex polyhedron and that condition (22) holds. The operator $B$ is supposed to be equal to $\nabla f$ for a convex function $f: H \to \mathbb{R}$ of class $\mathcal{C}^1$ satisfying $\inf f > -\infty$. The positive sequence $(\lambda_n)$ is assumed to be nondecreasing. Let $(x_n)$ be the sequence generated by the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ and let us set $x_\infty = s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_n$. Then there exists $n_* \in N$ such that:

$$
\forall n \geqslant n _ {*}, \quad x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \in N _ {A (0)} (- \nabla f (x _ {\infty})). \tag {23}
$$

Proof. Let $\xi_{n}$ be the unique element of $\partial \sigma_{A(0)}((x_{n + 2} - x_{n + 1}) / \lambda_n)$ such that:

$$
\begin{aligned} d \bigl (- (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} - \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}), \partial \sigma_ {A (0)} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}) \bigr) \\ &= \left| \xi_ {n} + (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}). \right| \\ \end{aligned}
$$

From the definitions of the term $\xi_{n}$ and the sequence $(x_{n})$, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned} \left| \xi_ {n} + (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda_ {n} ^ {2} + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) \right| \\ &\leqslant e \big (A ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}), \partial \sigma_ {A (0)} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda_ {n}) \big). \tag {24} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Since $s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_{n+2} - x_{n+1} = 0$ and the sequence $(\lambda_n)$ is minorized by some $\lambda > 0$, we infer from condition (22) that the right member of inequality (24) tends to 0 when $n \to +\infty$. Since $s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_{n+2} - 2x_{n+1} + x_n = 0$ and $s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} \nabla f(x_{n+2}) = \nabla f(x_\infty)$, we deduce from inequality (24) that $s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} \xi_n = -\nabla f(x_\infty)$. On the other hand, the fact that $\xi_n \in \partial \sigma_{A(0)}((x_{n+2} - x_{n+1}) / \lambda_n)$ is equivalent to $x_{n+2} - x_{n+1} \in N_{A(0)}(\xi_n)$. Finally, what remains to be seen is that $N_{A(0)}(\xi_n) \subset N_{A(0)}(-\nabla f(x_\infty))$ for $n$ large enough. Since $A(0)$ is a convex polyhedron, it suffices to apply property (21) with $C := A(0)$ and $\overline{x} := -\nabla f(x_\infty)$.

We now particularize the previous result to the important case of an operator A defined by $A := \partial\sigma_{C} + A_{0}$ (C convex set of H and $A_{0}: H \to H$ single-valued operator such that $\lim_{x \to 0} A_{0}(x) = 0$ ).

COROLLARY 5.1. Let $C$ be a closed convex polyhedron of $H$ which is bounded and such that $0 \in \operatorname{int}(C)$. Consider a singlevalued maximal monotone operator $A_0$ : $H \to H$ which satisfies $\lim_{x \to 0} A_0(x) = 0$. Define the maximal monotone operator $A$ : $H \Rightarrow H$ by $A := \partial \sigma_C + A_0$. The operator $B$ and the sequence $(\lambda_n)$ satisfy the same assumptions as in Proposition 5.1. Let $(x_n)$ be the sequence generated by the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ and let us set $x_\infty = s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_n$. Then there exists $n_* \in \mathbb{N}$ such that

$$
\forall n \geq n _ {*}, \quad x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \in N _ {C} (- \nabla f (x _ {\infty})).
$$

Proof. Since $\lim_{x\to 0}A_0(x) = 0$, we have $A_0(0) = 0$ and hence the set $A(0) = \partial \sigma_C(0) = C$ is a convex polyhedron. On the other hand, we have

$$
\begin{aligned} e \big (A (x), \partial \sigma_ {A (0)} (x) \big) &= e (\partial \sigma_ {C} (x) + A _ {0} (x), \partial \sigma_ {C} (x)) \\ &= \sup _ {\xi \in \partial \sigma_ {C} (x)} d (\xi + A _ {0} (x), \partial \sigma_ {C} (x)) \\ &\leqslant \sup _ {\xi \in \partial \sigma_ {C} (x)} | \xi + A _ {0} (x) - \xi | = | A _ {0} (x) |. \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Since $\lim_{x\to0}A_{0}(x)=0$ we can conclude that $\lim_{x\to0}e(A(x),\partial\sigma_{A(0)}(x))=0$, hence condition (22) holds, which allows to apply Proposition 5.1. ☐

When $H = R^{n}$, the balls $B_{1}$ and $B_{\infty}$ (cf. Examples 2.3 and 2.4) provide us with two standard examples of sets C satisfying the assumptions of Corollary 5.1.

# 5.2. ESTIMATION OF THE CONVERGENCE RATE

Let us recall that the sequence $(E_{n})$ defined by $E_{n} := \frac{1}{2\lambda_{n}^{2}} |x_{n+1} - x_{n}|^{2} + f(x_{n+1})$ is nonincreasing. We now introduce another energy-like sequence $(F_{n})$, as follows:

$$
F _ {n} = E _ {n} - f (x _ {\infty}) - \langle \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} \rangle . \tag {25}
$$

The decay rate of $(F_{n})$ will be computed in the proof of the next theorem (see inequality (42)). We are going to prove that under adequate conditions the convergence rate of $|x_{n+1}-x_{n}|$ toward 0 is majorized by some geometric sequence. This result is the discrete version of [8, Theorem 4.4] where the trajectory is shown to satisfy an exponential decay rate. However, the proof is much more technical than in the continuous case.

THEOREM 5.1. Let C be a closed convex polyhedron of H which is bounded and such that $0 \in \operatorname{int}(C)$. Consider a singlevalued maximal monotone operator $A_{0}: H \to H$ of class $C^{2}$. Assume that $A_{0}(0) = 0$ and that the differential operator $dA_{0}(0)$ at 0 verifies the coercivity condition

$$
\forall x \in H, \quad \langle d A _ {0} (0) x, x \rangle \geqslant m | x | ^ {2}, \tag {26}
$$

for some m > 0. Define the maximal monotone operator A: $H \Rightarrow H$ by $A := \partial \sigma_{C} + A_{0}$. The operator B is supposed to be equal to $\nabla f$ for a convex function $f: H \to R$ of class $C^{1}$ which is bounded from below. Assume that the function f is strongly convex, i.e., there exists M > 0 such that

$$
\forall x, y \in H, f (x) \geqslant f (y) + \langle \nabla f (y), x - y \rangle + M / 2 | x - y | ^ {2}. \tag {27}
$$

The sequence $(\lambda_{n})$ is assumed to be constant, equal to $\lambda > 0$. Let $(x_{n})$ be the sequence generated by the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ and let us set $x_{\infty} = s - \lim_{n \to +\infty} x_{n}$. Then, there exist $n_{0} \in N$, K > 0 and $q \in]0, 1[$ such that

$$
\forall n \geqslant n _ {0}, \quad | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | \leqslant K q ^ {n}. \tag {28}
$$

Proof. Throughout the proof, we denote by L the symmetric linear operator $dA_{0}(0)$ and we define the auxiliary sequence $(G_{n})$ by:

$$
G _ {n} := 1 / \lambda \bigl \langle x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}, x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} \bigr \rangle + 1 / 2 \langle L (x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} \rangle .
$$

An elementary computation shows that

$$
\begin{array}{l} G _ {n + 1} - G _ {n} = 1 / \lambda \langle x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}, x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle + 1 / \lambda \langle x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}, x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle + \\ + 1 / 2 \langle L (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle + 1 / 2 \langle L (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}), x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} \rangle \\ = 1 / \lambda \langle x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n} + \lambda L (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle + \\ + 1 / \lambda \langle x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}, x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle - 1 / 2 \langle L (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle . \\ \end{array}
$$

We finally obtain

$$
\begin{array}{l} G _ {n + 1} - G _ {n} = \lambda \big \langle (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} + A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) + \\ + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle - \\ - \lambda \langle A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) - L ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle - \\ - \lambda \langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle + \lambda \langle \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle + \\ + 1 / \lambda \langle x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}, x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle - \\ - 1 / 2 \langle L (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle . \tag {29} \\ \end{array}
$$

Let us evaluate each term of the right member of (29). Since $A = \partial \sigma_C + A_0$, we infer from (A) that

$$
\begin{aligned} - (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} - A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) - \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) \\ &\in \partial \sigma_ {C} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) \subset C. \tag {30} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Since the set $C$ is a convex polyhedron, we deduce from Corollary 5.1 that there exists $n_* \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $n \geqslant n_*$, $x_{n+1} - x_n \in N_C(-\nabla f(x_\infty))$. By summing these inclusions from $n+2$ to $+\infty$ and using the stability of the cone $N_C(-\nabla f(x_\infty))$ under addition, we obtain $x_\infty - x_{n+2} \in N_C (-\nabla f(x_\infty))$ for every $n \geqslant n_*$. We then deduce from inclusion (30) that for every $n \geqslant n_*$,

$$
\begin{aligned} \big \langle (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} + A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) + \\ + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle &\leqslant 0. \tag {31} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Since the function $A_{0}$ is of class $C^{2}$ and $A_{0}(0)=0$, we deduce from the Taylor expansion of $A_{0}$ at 0 that, when $n\to+\infty$

$$
\left| A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) - L ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) \right| = \mathrm{O} \Big (| x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} | ^ {2} \Big). \tag {32}
$$

Consider some $\eta \in ]0$, $m[$ (where $m$ is defined by (26)). Since $s - \lim_{n\to +\infty}x_n = x_\infty$, we deduce from the previous equality that for $n$ large enough

$$
\begin{aligned} \left| \left\langle A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) - L ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \right\rangle \right| \\ &\leqslant \frac {\eta}{\lambda^ {2}} \left| x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right| ^ {2}. \\ \end{aligned}
$$

On the other hand, the convexity of f yields

$$
\langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}), x _ {\infty} - x _ {n + 2} \rangle \leqslant f (x _ {\infty}) - f (x _ {n + 2}) \tag {34}
$$

In view of the coercivity condition on $L := \mathrm{d}A_{0}(0)$ we can write

$$
\langle L (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle \geqslant m | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} | ^ {2} \geqslant 0. \tag {35}
$$

Let us finally recall the classical inequality

$$
\left\langle x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}, x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right\rangle \leqslant 1 / 2 \left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \right| ^ {2} + 1 / 2 \left| x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right| ^ {2}. \tag {36}
$$

By combining equality (29) with equalities (31), (33), (34), (35) and (36), we obtain for n large enough:

$$
\begin{aligned} G _ {n + 1} - G _ {n} &\leqslant \frac {1 + 2 \eta}{2 \lambda} | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} | ^ {2} + \frac {1}{2 \lambda} | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | ^ {2} - \\ - \lambda (f (x _ {n + 2}) - f (x _ {\infty}) - \langle \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {\infty} \rangle). \\ \end{aligned}
$$

The last inequality can be re-expressed in terms of the sequence $(F_{n})$ (see definition (25)):

$$
G _ {n + 1} - G _ {n} + \lambda F _ {n + 1} \leqslant \frac {1 + \eta}{\lambda} | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} | ^ {2} + \frac {1}{2 \lambda} | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | ^ {2}. \tag {37}
$$

Let us compute the decay rate of the sequence $(F_{n})$

$$
\begin{aligned} F _ {n + 1} - F _ {n} &= \left\langle x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}, x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right\rangle / \lambda^ {2} - \\ - \left| x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n} \right| ^ {2} / (2 \lambda^ {2}) + f (x _ {n + 2}) - f (x _ {n + 1}) - \\ - \left\langle \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right\rangle . \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Let us rearrange the terms of the previous equality by taking into account the fact that $|x_{n+2} - 2x_{n+1} + x_n|^2 \geqslant 0$.

$$
\begin{array}{l} F _ {n + 1} - F _ {n} \leqslant \big \langle (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} + A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) + \\ + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle - \\ - \left\langle A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) - L ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right\rangle - \\ - \left\langle L (x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \right\rangle / \lambda + \\ + f (x _ {n + 2}) - f (x _ {n + 1}) - \langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle . \tag {38} \\ \end{array}
$$

Let us evaluate each term of the right member of inequality (38). Formula (30) can be equivalently rewritten as:

$$
\begin{aligned} x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} &\in N _ {C} \bigl (- (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} - A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) - \\ - \nabla f (x _ {n + 2})). \\ \end{aligned}
$$

Since $-\nabla f(x_{\infty}) \in C$, we infer from the previous inclusion that

$$
\begin{aligned} \big \langle (x _ {n + 2} - 2 x _ {n + 1} + x _ {n}) / \lambda^ {2} + A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) + \\ + \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}) - \nabla f (x _ {\infty}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle &\leqslant 0. \tag {39} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

On the other hand, we deduce from inequality (32) that

$$
\begin{aligned} \langle A _ {0} ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda) - L ((x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1}) / \lambda), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle \\ &= \mathrm{O} \Big (| x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} | ^ {3} \Big). \tag {40} \\ \end{aligned}
$$

The convexity of $f$ yields:

$$
f (x _ {n + 2}) - f (x _ {n + 1}) - \langle \nabla f (x _ {n + 2}), x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \rangle \leqslant 0. \tag {41}
$$

Finally, by combining inequality (38) with inequalities (35), (39), (40) and (41), we have

$$
F _ {n + 1} - F _ {n} \leqslant - \frac {m}{\lambda} \big | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \big | ^ {2} + \mathrm{O} \Big (\big | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} \big | ^ {3} \Big),
$$

which enables us to conclude that, for n large enough:

$$
F _ {n + 1} - F _ {n} \leqslant - \frac {m - \eta}{\lambda} | x _ {n + 2} - x _ {n + 1} | ^ {2}. \tag {42}
$$

By combining now inequalities (37) and (42), we obtain

$$
G _ {n + 1} - G _ {n} + \frac {1 + \eta}{m - \eta} (F _ {n + 1} - F _ {n}) + \frac {1 / 2}{m - \eta} (F _ {n} - F _ {n - 1}) + \lambda F _ {n + 1} \leqslant 0.
$$

Defining the sequence $(H_{n})$ by

$$
H _ {n} := G _ {n} + \frac {1 + \eta}{m - \eta} F _ {n} + \frac {1 / 2}{m - \eta} F _ {n - 1},
$$

we have:

$$
H _ {n + 1} - H _ {n} + \lambda F _ {n + 1} \leqslant 0. \tag {43}
$$

Let us now prove the following relation between the sequences $(F_{n})$ and $(G_{n})$ :

$$
\forall n \in \mathbb {N}, \quad G _ {n} \geqslant - F _ {n} / m \quad \text {and} \quad F _ {n} \geqslant \tau_ {0} G _ {n}, \tag {44}
$$

where $\tau_{0}$ is a positive real that we are going to determine. It is well known that, for all $\theta > 0$.

$$
| \langle x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n}, x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} \rangle | \leqslant \frac {| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | ^ {2}}{2 \theta} + \frac {\theta}{2} | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} | ^ {2}.
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
G _ {n} \geqslant - \frac {\left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \right| ^ {2}}{2 \lambda \theta} + \frac {- \theta / \lambda + m}{2} \left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} \right| ^ {2}, \tag {45}
$$

and

$$
G _ {n} \leqslant - \frac {\left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \right| ^ {2}}{2 \lambda \theta} + \frac {\theta / \lambda + \| L \|}{2} \left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} \right| ^ {2}, \tag {46}
$$

where $\|L\| := \sup_{|x| \leqslant 1} |Lx|$ is the norm of the continuous operator L.

Taking $\theta = \lambda m$ in inequality (45) we obtain

$$
G _ {n} \geqslant - \frac {\left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \right| ^ {2}}{2 \lambda^ {2} m} \geqslant - F _ {n} / m,
$$

which is the first inequality of (44). On the other hand, the strong convexity assumption (27) implies:

$$
F _ {n} \geqslant \frac {1}{2 \lambda^ {2}} | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} | ^ {2} + \frac {M}{2} | x _ {n + 1} - x _ {\infty} | ^ {2}. \tag {47}
$$

Setting

$$
\tau (\theta) := \min \biggl \{\theta / \lambda , \frac {M}{\theta / \lambda + \| L \|} \biggr \},
$$

we deduce from inequalities (46) and (47) that

$$
F _ {n} \geqslant \tau (\theta) G _ {n}. \tag {48}
$$

We let the reader check that the maximum value of the function $\tau\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}$ equals $\tau_{0}:=\left(\sqrt{\|L\|^{2}+4M}-\|L\|\right)/2$ and it is achieved at $0:=\lambda\tau_{0}$. Taking $\theta=\theta_{0}$ in inequality (48), we obtain the second inequality of (44).

From inequality (42), the sequence $(F_{n})$ is non-increasing and hence the expression of $(H_{n})$ combined with (44) yields

$$
\left(\frac {3 / 2 + \eta}{m - \eta} - \frac {1}{m}\right) F _ {n} \leqslant H _ {n} \leqslant \left(\frac {3 / 2 + \eta}{m - \eta} + \frac {1}{\tau_ {0}}\right) F _ {n - 1}. \tag {49}
$$

Setting $\alpha := (m - \eta)/(3/2 + \eta)$, we deduce from (43) and the second inequality of (49) that

$$
H _ {n + 1} - H _ {n} + \lambda \frac {\tau_ {0} \alpha}{\tau_ {0} + \alpha} H _ {n + 2} \leqslant 0. \tag {50}
$$

On the other hand, from the first inequality of (49), we deduce that

$$
H _ {n} \geqslant \frac {1}{2 m} F _ {n} \geqslant 0, \tag {51}
$$

which, in view of inequality (50) shows that the sequence $(H_{n})$ is nonincreasing. Using inequality (50) again, we obtain

$$
(\tau_ {0} + \alpha + \lambda \tau_ {0} \alpha) H _ {n + 2} \leqslant (\tau_ {0} + \alpha) H _ {n}.
$$

Consequently, there exists $K > 0$ such that for $n$ large enough:

$$
H _ {n} \leqslant K \bigg (\frac {\tau_ {0} + \alpha}{\tau_ {0} + \alpha + \lambda \tau_ {0} \alpha} \bigg) ^ {n / 2}.
$$

Finally, we can conclude, in view of inequality (51) that for $n$ large enough:

$$
\left| x _ {n + 1} - x _ {n} \right| \leqslant \sqrt {2 \lambda^ {2} F _ {n}} \leqslant \sqrt {4 \lambda^ {2} m K} \bigg (\frac {\tau_ {0} + \alpha}{\tau_ {0} + \alpha + \lambda \tau_ {0} \alpha} \bigg) ^ {n / 4}.
$$

Remark 5.1. In the statement of Theorem 5.1, the operator $A$ is supposed to be of the form $A = \partial \sigma_{C} + A_{0}$. It is possible to show that the conclusions of Theorem 5.1 hold true for more general operators $A$, provided that there exists a symmetric operator $L \in \mathcal{L}(H)$ such that

$$
e \big (A (x), \partial \sigma_ {A (0)} (x) + L x \big) = \mathrm{O} \Big (| x | ^ {2} \Big) \quad \text {when} x \to 0.
$$

The operator L must also verify the coercivity condition: $(Lx, x) \geqslant m|x|^{2}$ for some m > 0 and for every $x \in H$. The arguments of the proof are the same as previously and the details are left to the reader.

Remark 5.2. Estimate (28) of the discrete velocity $|x_{n+1} - x_n|$ allows to evaluate the distance of the iterate $x_n$ to the limit $x_\infty$. More precisely, we immediately deduce from inequality (28) that

$$
\forall n \geqslant n _ {0}, \quad | x _ {n} - x _ {\infty} | \leqslant \frac {K}{1 - q} q ^ {n}.
$$

Further Remarks. Other Possible Developments. We have proved that, under adequate conditions, the sequence $(x_{n})$ defined by $(\mathcal{A})$ finitely converges. For numerical and theoretical purposes, it would be interesting to determine the minimal number of iterations which is necessary to attain convergence. This number is clearly related to the distance $d(0, \operatorname{bd}(A(0)))$ and it must tend to $+\infty$ when $d(0, \operatorname{bd}(A(0)))$ tends to 0.

On the other hand, in the whole paper we have considered the algorithm $(\mathcal{A})$ which is an exact discretization of the associated continuous dynamical system. For numerical applications, it would be important to deal with approximate discretizations $(\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon})$, for example by considering $\varepsilon$ -enlargments of the operators A and B. These remarks certainly indicate directions for future investigation.
